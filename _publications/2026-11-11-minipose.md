Estimating the 6D pose of large, heavy industrial objects such as shipyard piping is important for automation, but collecting real training data is costly because the objects are hard to reposition, and synthetic data fails to capture the structured errors of real depth sensors. MiniPose addresses this by building a scaled-down replica of the object and capturing RGB-D images of it in varied poses. The RGB images are used directly, while the depth maps are transformed with an inverse scaling step so that they represent the full-scale object.

Evaluated with two RGB-D pose estimators, PVN3D and RDPN6D, adding no more than 1,000 miniature images per pipe type (under 10% of the training data) to 10,000 synthetic images improves mean ADD-S AUC over synthetic-only training, showing that miniature data is a practical stand-in for full-scale real data.
